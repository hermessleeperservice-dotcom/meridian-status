**AI Growth Lab sandbox opens its first cohort while the Bill itself stalls in Parliament**
The Regulating for Growth Bill had no fresh parliamentary movement this week, but its flagship mechanism went live when the government's AI Growth Lab opened applications for its first sector, legal services, with a window that 
lets companies trial AI products under supervision before taking them to market, with legal services going first and other sectors to follow, with a deadline of 27 September 2026
. So what: this is the template for how a future grocery-sector sandbox slot would work, so start scoping now what personalisation or loyalty experiment you'd want fast-tracked through regulatory relaxation once retail is invited in. [Legal Futures](https://www.legalfutures.co.uk/latest-news/legal-services-ai-growth-lab-opens-doors-for-applications)

**Tesco pilots AI agent that plans meals and builds baskets automatically**
Tesco has begun testing an agentic assistant, developed with UK AI consultancy Tomoro, that 
suggests recipe ideas based on a customer's preferences and dietary needs and can add the recommended ingredients directly into a shopper's basket, in beta with roughly 280,000 Tesco employees ahead of a wider customer rollout
. So what: this pushes personalisation past recommendation into autonomous action on a customer's behalf, meaning your UX must now design consent, override and "explainability" moments for a basket the shopper didn't build themselves. [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/retail/2026/brands-are-losing-shelf-space-to-ai-algorithms/)

**Tesco's agentic AI leans on 30 years of Clubcard data via Adobe and Mistral**
Tesco confirmed its agentic strategy is built on 
"agentic AI" that can take action, leveraging 30 years of Clubcard data through a partnership with Adobe to develop an AI assistant that offers personalized meal planning, food waste reduction advice, and automatically builds shopping baskets
, with Forbes noting 
the ultimate challenge for this autonomous retail vision will be earning customer trust
. So what: trust, not capability, is now the binding constraint on loyalty personalisation, so design work should prioritise transparency and easy correction over adding more automation. [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/bernardmarr/2026/08/12/tescos-ai-agents-could-soon-do-your-shopping-for-you/)

**Tesco finally goes nationwide on Uber Eats with Clubcard Prices attached**
Tesco has launched on Uber Eats across the UK for the first time, bringing Clubcard Prices to the rapid-delivery channel, with Uber Eats grocery lead Katie Hunter calling it a first-time launch that brings 
Tesco's much-loved product ranges to even more customers
. So what: loyalty pricing now has to work consistently across owned and third-party surfaces, so your personalisation logic and Clubcard price feeds need to be portable to aggregator UIs you don't fully control. [Retail Gazette](https://www.retailgazette.co.uk/blog/2026/08/tesco-launches-nationwide-on-uber-eats-with-clubcard-prices/)

**Sainsbury's pauses facial recognition after wrongly ejecting a loyal customer**
Sainsbury's suspended its Facewatch live facial recognition system at a London store after 
staff wrongly ejected a shopper in response to an alert, the second such incident this year
, even though the shopper had 
scanned his groceries and tapped his Nectar loyalty card
 at the time. So what: known, loyal customers can still be misidentified by store-level AI, a sharp reminder that identity systems feeding your personalisation stack need fail-safes that privilege first-party loyalty data over opaque biometric matches. [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/08/17/sainsburys-staff-eject-wrong-shopper-after-facial-recognition-alert-again/5288602)

**Iceland claims an 80% shrink cut from AI cameras, but the number needs scrutiny**
Iceland says its SAI-powered computer vision platform delivered 
an 80% reduction in losses since rollout, while the number of false alerts has fallen by almost 50 per cent
, though one analysis cautioned 
it is the retailer's own figure, published through a vendor announcement, with no independent audit and no baseline period stated, and an 80% cut in the worst decile is a different achievement from 80% estate-wide
. So what: expect loss-prevention AI headlines to keep setting inflated benchmarks for "AI ROI" that your personalisation and loyalty business cases will be measured against internally, so pre-empt comparisons with your own rigorously baselined metrics. [Grocery Gazette](https://www.grocerygazette.co.uk/2026/08/19/iceland-cuts-store-losses-by-80-with-ai-powered-theft-detection/) / [ResultSense](https://www.resultsense.com/news/2026-08-21-iceland-sai-shrink-facial-recognition/)
