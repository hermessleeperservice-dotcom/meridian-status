# Brief 2026-08-23

**Charity Majors says the real AI bottleneck has moved from code to judgment**
In a recorded conversation with The Pragmatic Engineer, Honeycomb's CEO argues the honest question is no longer whether engineers will ship code they haven't read but when, and 
the central argument is that as model intelligence crosses a threshold, the bottleneck in shipping software flips from execution to taste: product intuition, business judgment, and the courage to undertake far more ambitious work
. Anthropic's own internal shift is offered as evidence, where 
coding workflows moved from hesitant, permission-laden supervision to autonomous, multi-agent systems that now land 65% of all product-engineering pull requests
.
So what: as AI absorbs execution inside design and engineering alike, the scarce skill in your team stops being craft output and becomes editorial judgement, which should reshape what you hire and promote for.
https://finance.biggo.com/podcast/587a995ea52ede2a

**Peter Merholz names the real reason design struggles for budget**
Merholz argues design's chronic credibility problem isn't communication but "legibility," writing that when design asks for research budget it hears 
"You want to talk to 10 people? We already know what our customer wants"
 and when it presents findings it hears the opposite objection about sample size. He frames this as 
not simply a communication challenge but one requiring a fundamental understanding of the functions within a business, and the work that gets 'seen'
.
So what: if you're building the case for a group design function's headcount and research budget, frame it in terms of organisational legibility rather than design maturity, because that's the language the rest of the business actually rewards.
https://www.petermerholz.com/newsletter/designs-legibility-gap/

**Stratechery calls Anthropic's AI Act response a bad idea on principle**
Ben Thompson argues that 
Anthropic is adding watermarking in response to the EU's AI law, and it's a terrible idea, first and foremost for philosophical reasons
, rather than merely a technical compromise.
So what: watch how frontier labs respond to EU obligations, because their compliance choices (watermarking, provenance, disclosure) will filter down into the AI tooling and personalisation stacks your team is asked to integrate.
https://stratechery.com/2026/anthropics-watermarking-how-it-probably-works-worse-than-it-seems/

**Google's purchase of Spirit Airlines' data treated as evidence that data really is oil**
Covering Nvidia's latest deal alongside Anthropic's growth, Thompson notes 
Nvidia makes another deal, this time with a frontier lab, Anthropic's revenue continues to amaze, and maybe data finally is oil
, using an airline's customer data being acquired by a platform as the clearest sign of where value is shifting.
So what: it's a reminder that thirty years of transaction data is a strategic asset well beyond grocery, and the interesting competitive question is who else outside your sector is now buying or licensing behavioural data to build the same kind of moat.
https://stratechery.com/2026/nvidia-backs-openai-data-center-anthropic-news-google-buys-spirit-airlines-data/

**Noah Smith says AI just had its "Kasparov moment" for mathematics**
Smith describes a wave of LLMs producing 
counterexamples to significant long-standing conjectures
 within the space of a week, arguing this is the mathematical equivalent of Kasparov losing to a computer, a moment where a form of expert judgement long assumed safe stopped being safe.
So what: it's a useful essay for reframing internal conversations about "which parts of expert craft are actually defensible," including in research and design where the same argument gets made about taste and judgement.
https://www.noahpinion.blog/p/the-end-of-the-age-of-heroes

**Ethan Mollick's early Fable tests show what AI-run research analysis actually looks like**
Mollick reports the new frontier model working for hours unsupervised on research-style tasks, including 
a 19-page design document for a survey-answer analysis tool, executed over nine and a half hours, that turns open-ended text into defensible quantitative analysis with calibration, corrected statistics, and evidence inspection
. Elsewhere it 
worked up to roughly a dozen hours on multi-page specifications, launching helper AIs to research travel times and retrieving over 2,200 flights, rail schedules, and road-speed data
.
So what: this is the clearest signal yet that survey and qualitative-analysis pipelines, not just transcription, are becoming delegable, which should push you to pilot AI-run first-pass analysis on a real study rather than a toy one.
https://aichemyst.substack.com/p/give-fable-5-a-mission-not-a-just

**Lenny's second annual sentiment survey finds tech's workforce splitting in two over AI**
The latest edition of Lenny's ongoing tech-worker survey is titled 
"How tech workers are feeling in 2026: a workforce splitting in two,"
 tracking a widening gap between people who have adapted their workflow around AI and those who haven't.
So what: expect the same bifurcation inside your design and research teams, and treat closing that gap as a structural people problem, not a training-course problem.
https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/archive

**The ICO signals a statutory AI code and dedicated agentic-AI guidance are coming**
The regulator confirmed 
its 2026/27 AI work will include an AI code of practice, dedicated guidance on agentic AI, and more support for consumers dealing with increasingly personalised AI products
, while noting the fuller detail is still pending in a forthcoming strategy.
So what: agentic personalisation features (meal planning, basket-building, automated offers) are explicitly in scope for the next wave of ICO guidance, so build governance and human-oversight documentation now rather than retrofitting it later.
https://measuredcollective.com/ico-ai-code-of-practice-what-uk-organisations-should-do-before-the-next-strategy-lands/

**Hotel loyalty moves from segment-level offers to individual-level AI personalisation**
Industry analysis of 2026 hotel loyalty finds 
traditional loyalty personalisation operates at the segment level, business travellers get one set of offers, leisure travellers get another, while AI-powered personalisation in 2026 operates at the individual level, using booking history, stay behaviour, digital engagement patterns, and contextual data to tailor the loyalty experience to each guest
. Separately, hospitality data shows 
loyalty program membership grew by 14.5 percent in 2024
 as guests demand instant rather than deferred rewards.
So what: it's a useful outside-category benchmark for how far "individual-level" personalisation can go before it feels invasive, worth stress-testing against your own Clubcard-driven propositions.
https://bookingwhizz.com/en/blog/hotel-loyalty-trends-2026

**A rebuttal to the "95% of AI pilots fail" number reframes the real lesson as build-versus-buy**
Pushing back on the widely-cited MIT figure, one analysis notes 
the study's most important finding got buried, vendor-led deployments succeed 67% of the time while internal builds succeed one-third of the time, and this was always a story about strategy, not technology
. It also flags that 
29% of employees admit to sabotaging their company's AI strategy, rising to 44% among Gen Z, by entering proprietary data into public tools, using non-approved software, or tampering with performance metrics
.
So what: before another internal AI build gets greenlit inside design or research, ask whether a specialised vendor has already solved the integration and governance problem, and treat employee resistance as a rational signal worth investigating rather than a compliance failure to punish.
https://bsykes.substack.com/p/the-state-of-ai-adoption-in-the-enterprise
