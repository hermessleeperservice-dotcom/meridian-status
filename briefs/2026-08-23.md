# Brief 2026-08-23

**Design's legibility problem gets a sequel, not just a diagnosis**
Peter Merholz has followed his "legibility gap" post with a more prescriptive one, arguing that organisations value predictability because it lets them plan and budget, and that design's problem is a mismatch between how its work happens and how other functions expect work to be legible. He frames the fix as an ongoing translation job for design leaders rather than a one-off pitch. So what: this sharpens the budget argument beyond "design is undervalued" into "design's determinacy is illegible to functions that plan in Jira tickets and campaign spend," which is a more useful frame for the next resourcing conversation.
https://www.petermerholz.com/newsletter/bridging-the-legibility-gap/

**A subject access request turns a loyalty scheme inside out**
A Wired reporter requested his McDonald's loyalty data and got back 
a 515-page algorithmic dossier that doesn't just track his order history — it predicts what he'll buy next and concludes he'll never stop coming back
, framed as part of 
a $50 billion behavioral-prediction arms race disguised as rewards programs
. So what: this is the transparency test any AI-personalised loyalty scheme should expect a determined customer or journalist to run, and it's worth quietly rehearsing what your own subject access response would look like.
https://thewisemarketer.com/the-loyalty-newswire-august-17-2026/

**Loyalty is being redesigned for a machine customer, not a human one**
PYMNTS argues that as AI shopping agents start comparing prices and executing purchases on a person's behalf, 
the response, increasingly, is to stop designing loyalty programs for human feelings and start engineering them for computational legibility, making brand value queryable, parseable and optimizable in real time
. So what: if agents become a real channel, personalisation and offer design need a second interface that a model can parse cleanly, which is a genuinely new constraint for app, web and Clubcard-style experiences.
https://www.pymnts.com/artificial-intelligence-2/2026/how-brands-are-reinventing-loyalty-for-the-ai-decision-maker/

**Apple's EU settlement shows how little a regulator can actually extract**
Apple and the European Commission have settled their Digital Markets Act dispute, with Apple's standard App Store commission dropping only from 30% to 26%. Daring Fireball's read is blunt: 
this agreement is a sweeping victory for Apple — with the company conceding only the reduction from 30 to 26 percent for App Store payments
. So what: this is useful evidence for how slowly and incompletely EU platform regulation actually bites in practice, which matters when deciding how much app architecture or payment flow to build around assumptions of regulatory openness.
https://daringfireball.net/2026/08/apple_eu_business_terms_conceding_little

**Winning the A/B test isn't the same as making the right call**
Byrne Hobart's Diff makes the case that 
if AI wins A/B tests, it can still be a strategically sound decision not to use it
, alongside a related point that 
it's probably load-bearing that companies can turn money into changes in consumer perception
. So what: as AI-generated content and personalisation start winning short-run metrics, this is a useful check on whether "it tested better" is actually the right bar for brand and trust decisions in loyalty and CX.
https://www.thediff.co/archive/who-hedges-where/

**AI is moving faster than the institutions meant to govern it**
Nathan Lambert argues that recent AI-driven cyberattacks expose a mismatch between fast-moving AI labs and slow-moving government, writing that 
this scaling is pushing us towards new, inevitable AI transitions, which are accompanied by new risks... one that deserves its reputation as being slow-moving. This is a government that I expect to only act in substance once real, measurable harms from new AI models happen, and to overreact
. So what: this is a sharp frame for thinking about UK/EU AI regulation generally, including data and personalisation rules, likely to arrive reactively rather than ahead of the risk.
https://www.interconnects.ai/p/lessons-from-the-hacks

**The AI buildout's financing is starting to look like the risk itself**
Stratechery notes that 
Nvidia is finding new ways for its customers to raise money, and it's expanding the risk of the AI buildout significantly
. So what: as vendors increasingly finance their own customers' AI spend, it's worth treating AI capability announcements with a bit more scepticism about the underlying economics before they shape internal roadmaps.
https://stratechery.com/2026/nvidia-finds-new-ways-for-customers-to-raise-money/
