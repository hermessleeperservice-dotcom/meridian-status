# Brief 2026-08-26

**Merholz says the legibility problem in design never actually resolves**
In a new instalment building on his budget argument, Peter Merholz writes that 
organizations value predictability so they can plan and budget, and predictability requires determinacy, where the nature of work is understood ahead of time, which in turn defines legibility, the qualities of work that can be seen, appreciated, valued, and accounted for
. He adds that 
in the overwhelming majority of organizations, a design leader's work in this regard is never done
.
So what: reorganising the design function won't make the legibility problem disappear once and for all, it just resets the negotiation, so the group design function needs a permanent operating habit for translating design work into planning language rather than a one-off fix.
[Peter Merholz](https://www.petermerholz.com/newsletter/bridging-the-legibility-gap/)

**Charity Majors: unread AI code is fine if telemetry does the reviewing**
In a fuller writeup of her Pragmatic Engineer conversation, Charity Majors argues 
the honest question is no longer whether engineers will ship code they have not read, but when, and what must be true for that to become acceptable
, with observability replacing manual code review as the safety net.
So what: for a design function increasingly shipping AI-generated interface and personalisation logic, this reframes the QA question from "who reviewed this" to "what telemetry would catch it going wrong," which has direct implications for how research and evaluation practice needs to be redesigned.
[The Pragmatic Engineer](https://finance.biggo.com/podcast/587a995ea52ede2a)

**Interconnects predicts Anthropic's open culture won't survive its IPO**
In an interview on the state of open models, Nathan Lambert discusses 
why he expects Anthropic's famously open internal culture to break after its IPO
, alongside his skepticism that recursive self-improvement is close.
So what: it's a reminder that the vendors design teams are building AI features on top of are themselves mid-reorganisation, and their public commitments to openness and safety may not survive contact with public-market incentives.
[Interconnects](https://www.the-information-bottleneck.com/p/nathan-lambert-inside-post-training)

**ICO's children's-data crackdown shows where profiling enforcement is heading**
Under the UK's phased data reforms, 
the ICO has reviewed 34 social media and video-sharing platforms focusing on default privacy and geolocation settings, profiling for targeted advertising, and age assurance, and has already fined two platforms a combined total of over £14.7 million for shortcomings in handling children's data
.
So what: the enforcement pattern (default settings, profiling for advertising, age assurance) is a preview of where scrutiny of any consumer-facing personalisation engine is likely to go next, loyalty and app personalisation included.
[CMS Law](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/uk-data-protection-what-s-changed-what-s-next)

**Stratechery: Nvidia's financing deals are quietly expanding AI's systemic risk**
Ben Thompson argues that 
Nvidia is finding new ways for its customers to raise money, and it's expanding the risk of the AI buildout significantly
.
So what: as more of the AI capability a retailer buys or rents sits on top of vendor balance sheets stretched by circular financing, procurement and continuity risk for AI-dependent personalisation and research tooling deserves the same scrutiny as any other supplier concentration risk.
